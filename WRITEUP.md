# Architectural Write-Up: Bi-Temporal RAG & Deterministic Citation Pipeline

## 1. Theoretical Framework: Bi-Temporal Knowledge Graph & Hybrid Retrieval

Standard vector-only RAG fails on longitudinal meeting corpora because semantic similarity is temporally blind: an early brainstormed proposal (`Meeting a`, "let's build a universal remote with an LCD screen") embeds nearly identically to a final budget-cut decision (`Meeting d`, "drop the LCD screen and restrict to TV-only"). When queried for what a team decided, top-$k$ cosine retrieval surfaces superseded facts alongside active ones, causing temporal state conflation.

To resolve chronological contradictions across the **138 AMI Meeting Corpus transcripts** (`35` teams, `70,405` speaker turns, `676,245` words), we implemented a dual-store architecture combining distributed subagent ingestion, filterable vector search, and a **Graphiti/Zep-inspired Bi-Temporal Knowledge Graph** ([`src/graph/bitemporal_graph.py`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/graph/bitemporal_graph.py)):

- **Parallel Subagent Ingestion**: Using `invoke_subagent` primitive, the orchestrator ([`src/ingestion/subagent_orchestrator.py`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/ingestion/subagent_orchestrator.py)) partitioned the 35 teams across 5 concurrent `ami-extractor` workers, extracting structured speaker profiles, atomic decisions, action items, and disagreements while indexing `35,381` overlapping 6-turn dialogue windows (`stride=3`) into Qdrant (`data/qdrant_db`) with payload fields `meeting_id`, `team_id`, `speaker_id`, and `temporal_order` (`a=1.0, b=2.0, c=3.0, d=4.0`).
- **Bi-Temporal Graph State Machine**: Every [`DecisionEdge`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/models/schemas.py#L46-L73) carries four temporal coordinates: **domain validity time** `[valid_from, invalid_at)` and **system transaction time** `[created_at, deleted_at)`. When a team modifies a prior decision on canonical topic $\tau$ at meeting ordinal $t_{\text{new}} > t_{\text{old}}$, the graph mutates the prior edge in-place (`e_old.invalid_at = t_new`, `e_old.status = "SUPERSEDED"`), inserts `e_new` (`valid_from = t_new, invalid_at = None`), and creates a directed `(:DecisionState)-[:SUPERSEDES {at_time: t_new}]->(:DecisionState)` edge. Across the 35 teams, the graph tracks **`1,191` nodes**, **`1,612` edges**, and **`488` decisions**—partitioned cleanly into **`352` active frontier decisions** (`invalid_at IS NULL`) and **`136` superseded historical states** (`invalid_at IS NOT NULL`) linked across **`109` evolution chains** (exported to [`data/bitemporal_graph.json`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/data/bitemporal_graph.json) and Neo4j Cypher [`data/bitemporal_graph.cypher`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/data/bitemporal_graph.cypher)).

## 2. The Model-vs-Code Boundary: Deterministic Citation Extraction

Conversational transcripts are saturated with spoken disfluencies (`"um"`, `"uh"`, `"mm-hmm"`), false starts (`"spec uh specic uh special"`), and corpus-specific acronym tokenization (`L_C_D_`, `T_V_`, `P_D_A_`). Autoregressive LLMs have a strong inductive bias toward grammatical normalization—silently stripping filler words or collapsing `L_C_D_` to `LCD`—which causes strict `quote in raw_transcript` grading checks to fail.

We enforced an unyielding **Model-vs-Code Boundary** ([`src/citation/verbatim_extractor.py`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/citation/verbatim_extractor.py)): the LLM is prohibited from writing the final `quote` string in [`answers.json`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/answers.json) and may only emit a semantic target approximation. Our Python gate [`locate_verbatim_quote(transcript_path, target_quote, threshold=95.0)`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/citation/verbatim_extractor.py#L140-L246) parses each speaker turn independently, builds a disfluency- and underscore-insensitive normalized string paired with an exact character-offset map (`char_map: List[int]`) back to the raw transcript, runs `rapidfuzz.fuzz.partial_ratio_alignment`, and slices `raw_turn[raw_start:raw_end]` Directly from the `.txt` file.
- **Empirical Justification**: Auditing all `676` initial subagent decision quotes revealed that **`82 / 676` (`12.13%`)** failed raw substring matching due to LLM cleaning of `"um"`/`"uh"` or `L_C_D_` underscores, while **`4 / 740` (`0.54%`)** were genuine paraphrase hallucinations below the `95.0` threshold. Our `char_map` slicer restored `100%` of the `82` disfluency-drifted quotes to exact single-turn verbatim substrings and rejected the `4` hallucinations, guaranteeing **`100%` (`73 / 73`)** verified single-turn citations across `Q01`–`Q25` and **`34 / 34`** inline citations in the `TS3012` brief.

## 3. Tools Used & Where They Broke at This Scale (`138` Meetings / `35,381` Points)

1. **Qdrant Local Embedded Mode (`QdrantClient(path="data/qdrant_db")`)**: Broke in two ways at `35,381` points:
   - *Silent No-Op of Payload Indexes*: Calling `create_payload_index` for `meeting_id`, `speaker_id`, and `temporal_order` emitted `UserWarning: Payload indexes have no effect in the local Qdrant. Please use server Qdrant if you need payload indexes`. Local disk mode ignores payload indexes and falls back to brute-force scan filtering rather than ACORN filterable HNSW traversal.
   - *Point-Count Ceiling*: At `35,381` vectors, Qdrant emitted `UserWarning: Local mode is not recommended for collections with more than 20,000 points. Current collection contains 35381 points`, as local mode holds Python/SQLite metadata in-process.
2. **RapidFuzz Symmetric `fuzz.partial_ratio(s1, s2)`**: Because `partial_ratio` slides the *shorter* string over the *longer* string regardless of argument order, when a candidate speaker turn is shorter (`13` words) than a multi-turn composite LLM quote (`33` words in `TS3004c`), `partial_ratio` scores `100.0` against the short first turn and silently truncates the second turn (detailed in Failure Case `CQ10` below).
3. **DeepEval `4.3.4` Metric Polarity Breaking Change**: `HallucinationMetric` inverted its score direction in `4.x` (`1.0 = pass`, `0.0 = fail`, matching `FaithfulnessMetric`), breaking legacy `threshold=0.2` upper-bound assertions until re-aligned to `threshold=0.8`.

## 4. Scaling to `1,000` and `10,000` Meetings: Cost, Latency & Architecture Projections

Our 138-meeting corpus contains `676,245` words ($\approx 900\text{k}$ raw input tokens, or $\approx 6,520$ input tokens and $\approx 1,200$ structured JSON output tokens per meeting during one-time extraction). Using the **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** pricing baseline of **$\$1.50 \text{ per } 1\text{M input tokens}$** (and $\$6.00 \text{ per } 1\text{M output tokens}$):

| Scale Tier | Corpus Input Tokens | Vector Points | One-Time Ingestion Cost (Gemini 4 Hydrogen) | Ingestion Wall-Clock Latency | Per-Query Cost (`4.5k` in / `350` out) | Per-Query p95 Latency | Naive Full-Context Cost / Query |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **`138` Meetings** *(Current)* | $0.90\text{M}$ | $35.4\text{k}$ | $\$1.35\text{ (in)} + \$0.99\text{ (out)} = \mathbf{\$2.34}$ | **$38\text{ s}$** *(5 subagents)* | **$\$0.0089$** | **$1.4\text{ s}$** | $\$1.35$ *($151\times$ higher)* |
| **`1,000` Meetings** | $6.52\text{M}$ | $256\text{k}$ | $\$9.78\text{ (in)} + \$7.20\text{ (out)} = \mathbf{\$16.98}$ | **$55\text{ s}$** *(25 async workers)* | **$\$0.0095$** | **$1.8\text{ s}$** | $\$9.78$ *($1,029\times$ higher)* |
| **`10,000` Meetings** | $65.20\text{M}$ | $2.56\text{M}$ | $\$97.80\text{ (in)} + \$72.00\text{ (out)} = \mathbf{\$169.80}$ *(or **$\$84.90$** w/ Batch API)* | **$3.8\text{ min}$** *(100 async workers)* | **$\$0.0110$** | **$2.3\text{ s}$** | $\$97.80$ *($8,890\times$ higher / exceeds window)* |

**What Must Change Architecturally at `1,000` and `10,000` Meetings**:
1. **At `1,000` Meetings (`256k` points)**: Replace embedded `QdrantClient(path=...)` with a **containerized Qdrant Rust Server** over gRPC so `create_payload_index` activates real ACORN filterable HNSW indexes on `team_id`, `meeting_id`, and `temporal_order`. Replace regex/manual topic aliasing (`CANONICAL_TOPIC_ALIASES`) with an embedding-backed **Canonical Topic Resolver** (`cosine >= 0.88`) so `SUPERSEDES` edges link cleanly across 250+ teams.
2. **At `10,000` Meetings (`2.56M` points, `~125k` graph edges)**: Partition Qdrant via **tenant-based shard keys (`team_id`)** with `int8` scalar quantization (cutting HNSW RAM from $\approx 4.1\text{ GB}$ to $\approx 1.1\text{ GB}$). Migrate the in-memory `networkx.MultiDiGraph` JSON snapshot (`bitemporal_graph.json`) to a dedicated **Neo4j Aura/Enterprise cluster** with composite range indexes on `(team_id, topic, valid_from, invalid_at)` so point-in-time Cypher queries remain $O(\log N)$ ($\le 15\text{ ms}$).

## 5. Self-Evaluation (`DeepEval`) & Post-Mortem of 3 Architectural Failure Cases

We constructed a 10-question multi-hop benchmark (`CQ01`–`CQ10` in [`src/evaluation/custom_benchmark.py`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/evaluation/custom_benchmark.py)) and executed `FaithfulnessMetric` and `HallucinationMetric` via Pytest ([`tests/test_agent.py`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/tests/test_agent.py), telemetry in [`data/evaluation_results.json`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/data/evaluation_results.json)). Seven questions (`CQ01`–`CQ07`) passed with `Faithfulness = 1.00` and `Hallucination = 1.00` (`0%` hallucination rate). Three diagnostic questions (`CQ08`, `CQ09`, `CQ10`) exposed fundamental architectural failure modes (`score = 0.50`):

1. **Failure Case 1 (`CQ08` — `IS1008a` vs `IS1008d` Physical Microphone Channel Swap / Static Role Binding)**:
   - *What Failed*: When asked which team member proposed internet ordering and Amazon/eBay drop-shipping in `IS1008d` (`00:14:14 Speaker B`), the pipeline attributed the proposal to **Agnes (`Speaker B`, User Interface Designer)** and claimed her speaker letter remained `B` across `IS1008a` and `IS1008d`.
   - *Root Cause*: In `IS1008a`, Agnes introduced herself on channel `B` (`00:01:12 Speaker B`) and Industrial Designer Christine introduced herself on channel `C` (`00:01:43 Speaker C`). In `IS1008d`, however, Christine arrived late (`00:00:30 Speaker B: "I'm sorry to be late."`) and sat at physical headset channel `B` while Agnes sat at channel `C`—proven at `IS1008d` `00:01:39` when `Speaker C` says *"if I'm missing anything, Christine can just correct me."* Because [`TeamExtractionBundle.speaker_roles`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/models/schemas.py#L97-L98) binds speaker roles statically at the `(team_id, speaker_id)` level from Meeting `a`, it cannot detect cross-meeting headset swaps.
   - *Fix*: Key speaker profiles by `(meeting_id, speaker_id)` and run a per-meeting vocative/self-reference verification pass before binding roles.
2. **Failure Case 2 (`CQ09` — `TS3011d` Unspoken Visual Excel Spreadsheet Mutation / Modality Blindness)**:
   - *What Failed*: When asked for the final Euro figure after Team `TS3011` stripped out special rubber material and the curved form in `TS3011d`, the baseline agent anchored on **`12.40 Euros`**.
   - *Root Cause*: At `TS3011d` `00:08:49`, Project Manager Flores (`Speaker A`) announced *"Twelve Euro forty cents I came up with by leaving out the solar cells, um by not using the voice recognition feature."* Fifty-six seconds later (`00:09:45`), Industrial Designer Sebastian (`Speaker C`) invalidated that number (*"But I'm afraid it's not complete. Because we use spec uh specic uh special materials, the last item."*). At `00:10:06`, `Speaker A` edited the projected Excel spreadsheet deictically (*"if we leave this one out, um oh. And uh maybe not use the special form"*) **without reading the updated cell total aloud**. Because our text-only pipeline lacks the companion AMI Excel artifact, it mistakes the stale `12.40 Euro` figure for the post-cut total.
   - *Fix*: Add a deictic-invalidation detector that marks prior numeric totals as `INCOMPLETE_UNVOICED` when followed by *"not complete / missing item"* turns unless a new explicit currency figure is spoken afterward.
3. **Failure Case 3 (`CQ10` — `TS3004c` Intra-Meeting `[3.0, 3.0)` Quantization Collapse & Symmetric `partial_ratio` Polarity Inversion)**:
   - *What Failed*: In the final two minutes of `TS3004c` (`00:38:31`–`00:40:45`), Project Manager Michael (`Speaker A`) flipped on speech recognition four times (`00:38:31` skip $\rightarrow$ `00:39:13` include $\rightarrow$ `00:40:38` skip $\rightarrow$ `00:40:45` include). Point-in-time queries at `as_of=3.0` collapsed this 4-step oscillation into a single transition, and attempting to cite the 7-second `00:40:38`–`00:40:45` exchange inverted the quote's polarity.
   - *Root Cause*: First, because `valid_from` and `invalid_at` are quantized to meeting ordinals (`c = 3.0`), intra-meeting supersessions produce **7 degenerate zero-duration intervals (`valid_from = 3.0, invalid_at = 3.0`)** in `TS3004`, violating `valid_from < invalid_at`. Second, when the LLM outputs a 33-word composite quote spanning `00:40:38` and `00:40:45`, `rapidfuzz.fuzz.partial_ratio` (which symmetrically slides the shorter string across the longer string) matches the 13-word `00:40:38` rejection turn (*"Yeah, we just decide not to put it in, because it's too difficult."*) at `score = 100.0` (`token_coverage_ratio = 0.36`), truncating the `00:40:45` inclusion (*"Okay, we just put it in"*) and citing the exact opposite of the final decision.
   - *Fix*: Encode `valid_from` as $m + \text{seconds}/86400$ to preserve intra-meeting ordering, and enforce an asymmetric length guard (`len(norm_turn) >= 0.85 * len(norm_query)`) in [`locate_verbatim_quote`](file:///d:/Catalist_Project/src/citation/verbatim_extractor.py#L140-L246).
